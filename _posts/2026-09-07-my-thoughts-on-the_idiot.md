Since the start of my life, I have only managed to read one book by Fyodor Dostoyevsky. It's the classic everyone grabs. "Crime and punishment". After three or so years after reading it, I could finally gain the courage to pick one more book of his.
I am still in the process of finishing it but at the same time, I want to also talk about what passages or concepts stayed with me. Life doesn't have to be complicated as people say but is it not worth it to go into the depths of human mind and human society to see how it works, its flaws and triumphs.
The incident described in the book that has immensely moved me is the witnessing of an execution by the protagonist Myskin. The person at the brink of his life's end feels every second as infinite. He feels the sun on his face, he feels the warmth and admires it to the fullest. Every small thing that 
is taken for granted in daily life feels magical and he wishes and thinks if he gets to live through this or if he gets a second chance, he will achnowledge every second of his life. This last statment is again scrutenized by the writer and confirmed that even if he is given a second chance or anyone else for that matter
they'll take their life for granted. The sunlight on face would just be a banality. This scene has come up in many movies and discussions and thoughts of many a people but human nature as such cannot 
change. We wake up do our daily routine without getting excited. We do not see the world around us in awe, mostly we don't. We live like we will live forever. Such is our nature. Maybe the only way out is to notice the daily mundane and teach oneself to do so actively but it is not ingrained in us to so and that is what makes it scary.
